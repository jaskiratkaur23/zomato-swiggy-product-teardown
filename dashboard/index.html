For most of the last decade, "Zomato vs. Swiggy" meant the same race: two food delivery apps, racing for the same customer, the same restaurant, the same scooter at the same red light.

Eight quarters of public data tells a different story now. Both food delivery businesses look identical on paper — same growth, same margin. The race they were really running is hiding inside their second bet.

By March 2026, this share of Eternal's total B2C transaction volume was no longer food delivery. It was Blinkit.

52%

At Swiggy, the same number is 47%. The five-point difference doesn't sound like much. It is the whole argument.

MOVEMENT ONE

The race everyone still talks about is the one that already ended in a draw.

Through FY23 and most of FY24, Zomato's food delivery business held a clear operational lead — faster margin expansion, better unit economics, the smarter operator narrative. That gap has closed completely.

By Q4 FY26, Zomato earns 3.4% EBITDA on every rupee of food delivery GOV. Swiggy earns 3.3%. The two grow at near-identical rates. There is no longer an "operator quality" argument to be made about food delivery.

Swiggy's food delivery margin caught up to Zomato's, and then they both stopped moving.

Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of food delivery GOV, by quarter.

Both businesses now run at roughly the same scale-adjusted profitability. The food delivery gap that used to define this comparison has flattened into a rounding error. Whatever wins the next decade for either company is not going to come from food.

MOVEMENT TWO

Quick commerce is the bet at one company. At the other, it's still a side project.

While the food delivery numbers were converging, both companies were quietly pouring capital into their quick-commerce arms — Blinkit for Eternal, Instamart for Swiggy. The two businesses have grown at similar percentage rates. The absolute scale has not.

The line where Blinkit overtook Zomato food delivery is the line where these two companies became different categories.

Total B2C GOV by quarter, food delivery + quick commerce, stacked. Eternal pair on the left, Swiggy pair on the right within each quarter.

Look at the Eternal pair on the right of each group. In Q1 FY25, the brick-colored Blinkit stack is the smaller half of the bar. By Q1 FY26, it's the larger half. By Q4 FY26, it's not close.

Now look at the Swiggy pair. The brick is bigger every quarter — but it never crosses the line. The Swiggy company still gets most of its B2C transaction volume from food delivery. The Eternal company doesn't.

MOVEMENT THREE

Inside the quick-commerce bet, the story is more interesting than "Blinkit is winning."

Blinkit has positive contribution margin. Instamart does not. That part of the popular narrative is correct. But the part that says Instamart is falling further behind is wrong — at least on the unit-economics curve. The gap between the two has been narrowing for six straight quarters, and Instamart is the one doing the work to close it.

Instamart is bleeding less every quarter — but it's still bleeding.

Contribution margin as % of GOV, by quarter. The dashed line is breakeven.

The gap was 9.3 percentage points at its widest, in Q2 FY25. It now sits at 5.3. Instamart improved its contribution margin by 4.0 points in eight quarters; Blinkit improved by 1.1.

This doesn't mean Swiggy is winning. It means the popular shorthand of "Blinkit profitable, Instamart broken" is missing the actual operating reality: one of these businesses is working hard on the unit-level problem and making real progress. It's just doing so from below the waterline.

Both companies' dark stores are converging on the same number — around ₹7 crore of GOV per store per quarter.

Looks like a physical ceiling. Same 90-day window, same 2-kilometer catchment, same Indian Tier 1 demand density.

Two opposite trends, both ending in the same place. Blinkit's per-store output declined as it added 1,300 new stores into less dense neighborhoods. Instamart's climbed as it stopped expanding and let the network mature.

The fact that they meet at ~₹7 cr suggests this number isn't an operator choice. It's the demand inside a 2-kilometer radius of a dark store in an Indian Tier 1 city. That ceiling, once acknowledged, becomes the next strategic problem either company has to solve — through automation, larger stores, or smarter SKU mix.

ETERNAL

A quick-commerce company with a profitable food delivery business attached. 52% of B2C GOV is Blinkit. Stock has rerated to all-time highs. The parent renamed itself.

SWIGGY

A food-delivery company with a struggling quick-commerce business attached. 47% of B2C GOV is Instamart. Stock sits 44% below its 52-week high. Quick commerce reframed as a "deliberate slowdown."

THREE THINGS TO LEAVE WITH
  1. Food delivery in India is now a converged two-player commodity. Same growth, same margin, same MTU trajectory. Neither company can win the future by being better at food delivery — that race has ended.
  2. Inside quick commerce, the headline narrative is half right. Blinkit has the better economics; Instamart has been improving them faster. The race isn't margin — it's scale. Blinkit's absolute GOV lead widens every quarter, even as the contribution-margin gap closes.
  3. The single decision that defines this comparison was made roughly five quarters ago: Eternal made quick commerce its main bet and structured the company around it (separate apps, separate P&Ls, parent rename, public-market communication). Swiggy kept Instamart as a tab inside a super-app. That choice now explains the entire valuation gap.
