# Analysis: Zomato vs. Swiggy — Two Bets, One Industry

> 8 quarters of public data. Two business lines. Three findings ordered by how surprised I was by each.

---

## The framing problem

"Zomato vs Swiggy" is the most common comparison in Indian tech — but it's a question people answer using 2020 mental models. Both companies started as food delivery platforms. Today, they're holding companies running two big B2C bets each:

| | Eternal (parent of Zomato) | Swiggy Ltd |
|---|---|---|
| Food delivery brand | Zomato | Swiggy |
| Quick commerce brand | Blinkit | Instamart |
| Going-out brand | District | Scenes / Dineout |
| Other | Hyperpure (B2B) | Genie (errands) |

So "Zomato vs Swiggy" the question really has two layers:
1. How do the food delivery apps compare? *(The recognizable comparison.)*
2. How do the parent companies' total strategies compare? *(Where the strategic action is.)*

Layer 1 turns out to be the less interesting question.

---

## Finding 1 — Food delivery has fully converged

Eight quarters of food delivery GOV and EBITDA margin, side by side:

| Quarter | Zomato GOV (₹ cr) | Swiggy GOV (₹ cr) | Ratio | Zomato margin | Swiggy margin | Margin gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY25 | 9,264 | 6,298 | 1.47× | 2.5% | 0.2% | 2.3 pp |
| Q4 FY25 | 9,778 | 7,347 | 1.33× | 3.2% | 2.9% | 0.3 pp |
| Q4 FY26 | 11,990 | 9,005 | 1.33× | 3.4% | 3.3% | 0.1 pp |

Two patterns to read here:

**Scale gap stable at ~1.33×.** Zomato has held a ~33% lead in absolute GOV for six quarters straight. Both grow at near-identical rates (22–23% YoY in Q4 FY26). Neither is catching up; neither is pulling away.

**Margin gap effectively zero.** Swiggy closed a 2.3pp gap to 0.1pp. In four years (FY23 → FY26), Swiggy went from losing money on every order to making the same money per order as Zomato.

The implication: **Food delivery is now a stable two-player commodity business in India.** Same customer base, same restaurant supply, same unit economics, same growth rate. Whatever advantages either side has are too small to show up in quarterly disclosed metrics.

This is genuinely surprising. The popular narrative in 2022–23 framed Zomato as the smarter operator. By Q4 FY26 the data says: not anymore — Swiggy caught up entirely.

### Why this matters strategically

If food delivery margins are converged at ~3.3–3.4% of GOV and growth is converged at ~22%, then neither company can win the future by being better at food delivery. The race has moved.

---

## Finding 2 — Quick commerce is where the divergence lives

If food delivery is the converged business, quick commerce is the divergent one. Same operators, completely different operational realities:

| Quarter | Blinkit GOV | Instamart GOV | Blinkit CM | Instamart CM | CM Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY25 | 4,923 | 2,724 | +2.4% | -5.8% | 8.2 pp |
| Q2 FY25 | 6,132 | 3,382 | +2.9% | -6.4% | 9.3 pp |
| Q4 FY25 | 9,421 | 4,670 | +0.9% | -4.2% | 5.1 pp |
| Q4 FY26 | 13,232 | 7,881 | +3.5% | -1.8% | 5.3 pp |

Three observations stacked together:

1. **Blinkit is bigger and getting bigger faster in absolute terms.** Q4 FY26 GOV gap is ₹5,351 cr — almost the size of Instamart's *entire* quarterly GOV from Q1 FY25.
2. **Blinkit has positive contribution margin; Instamart doesn't.** Blinkit is the only QC operator in India that makes money per order.
3. **But the margin gap is *narrowing*.** From 9.3 pp peak (Q2 FY25) to 5.3 pp now. Instamart improved 4.0 pp in 8 quarters; Blinkit improved 1.1 pp. **Instamart is improving 3.6× faster** — just from below the waterline.

This last point matters. The standard read is "Blinkit winning, Instamart losing" — and on absolute scale that's right. But on improvement *rate*, Instamart is the one accelerating. Whether that closes the gap or just slows the bleeding depends on whether Instamart can sustain margin improvement while accelerating GOV growth — a much harder problem than improving margins on a shrinking business.

### A subtler signal: dark store productivity converging

GOV per dark store per quarter:

| Quarter | Blinkit ₹cr/store | Instamart ₹cr/store |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY25 | 7.70 | 4.89 |
| Q4 FY25 | 7.24 | 4.57 |
| Q4 FY26 | 6.96 | 6.90 |

Two opposite trends crossing. Blinkit's per-store output is *declining* (pushing into less dense neighborhoods → lower order density per store). Instamart's is *rising* (stopped opening new stores, let existing ones mature). The two converge near ~₹7 cr/store/quarter.

This looks like a real ceiling — possibly the natural limit of demand within a 2km catchment in Indian Tier 1 cities. Whoever cracks past this number first (automation, larger stores, smarter SKU mix) opens the next leg of differentiation.

## Finding 3 — Eternal is becoming a quick-commerce company; Swiggy isn't

This is the chart I'd lead with in any exec presentation:

| Q4 FY26 B2C GOV | Eternal | Swiggy Ltd |
|---|---|---|
| Food delivery | ₹11,990 cr (48%) | ₹9,005 cr (53%) |
| Quick commerce | ₹13,232 cr (52%) | ₹7,881 cr (47%) |
| **Total B2C GOV** | **₹25,222 cr** | **₹16,886 cr** |

Eight quarters ago, both companies looked similar — food delivery was the majority business, QC was the bet on the side. By Q4 FY26:

- **At Eternal, Blinkit is now the bigger business** (52% of B2C GOV). Quick commerce passed food delivery in Q3 FY26.
- **At Swiggy, Instamart is still the second business** (47%). Food delivery still leads.

Two companies that started identical have ended up in different categories. Eternal is now best described as a quick-commerce company with a profitable food delivery business attached. Swiggy is still a food-delivery company with a struggling quick-commerce business attached.

This is the strategic decision that explains everything else:

- Why Eternal's stock has rerated to new highs and Swiggy's is 44% below its peak.
- Why Eternal renamed itself ("Eternal" is brand-neutral; "Zomato" was food-delivery-locked).
- Why Eternal split into separate apps (Blinkit deserves its own brand, P&L, and identity).
- Why Swiggy is now reframing Instamart's slowdown as "deliberate" — they may be right operationally, but strategically they're conceding the bigger bet.

## What this means for each company

### For Eternal (Zomato + Blinkit)

Two risks the data flags:

1. **Blinkit's per-store productivity is declining.** Continuing to add stores at the current pace will compound this. Marginal store ROI eventually turns negative.
2. **Food delivery margin advantage has evaporated.** Eternal can no longer claim "we run food delivery better than them." Both run it the same way.

### For Swiggy

The data supports two opportunities:

1. **The quality-over-quantity QC strategy is working at the unit level.** Margin convergence is real. AOV is rising faster than Blinkit's. Bottom line is moving in the right direction.
2. **Food delivery is fully competitive with Zomato.** Swiggy can no longer be dismissed as "the weaker food delivery brand."

And two risks:

1. **Absolute scale gap widens as unit-economics gap closes.** Worst possible combination: appearing to catch up while falling further behind in cash generation.
2. **No committed timeline to QC breakeven.** Public market patience for "we'll get there" runs out eventually.

## What I'd build next (if I had internal data access)

The interview answer:

1. **Cohort retention curves.** Of users acquired in Q1 FY25, what percentage are still ordering in Q4 FY26? At what frequency? Single most important metric for either business; neither discloses.
2. **SKU-mix contribution split.** Which QC categories (pharmacy, electronics, beauty, premium groceries) drive margin improvement? Both decks treat this as a blob.
3. **Tier 1 vs Tier 2 store P&L.** At what stage does a Tier 2 dark store become contribution-positive? Determines whether QC expansion math works at all.
4. **Ad/placement revenue isolation.** FMCG brand placement on QC platforms is rumored to be 12–18% of QC revenue at 70%+ margins. Neither company splits this. Doing so would reprice both businesses.
5. **Hourly demand modeling.** QC has obvious peak hours. Are dark stores sized for peak or for average? The answer drives the entire economic model.

## Caveats and honest limits

Flagging weaknesses is what separates analytics from advocacy.

- **8 quarters is a short series.** A single festive-season disruption could break these trends.
- **Disclosed metrics aren't perfectly like-for-like.** Both companies define adjusted EBITDA, contribution margin, and MTU slightly differently. I've used the most consistent series where available; some triangulation is unavoidable.
- **Early-period figures (Q1–Q2 FY25) involve more estimation than late-period.** Swiggy began disclosing several metrics consistently only after its IPO. Affected cells flagged in `data/SOURCES.md`.
- **Three-player market not fully modeled.** Zepto is the third QC operator, plus Flipkart Minutes and Tata BigBasket Now are scaling. I've focused on the two listed players for data-availability reasons. A complete picture would include all five.
- **Food delivery margin convergence could be coincidence, not destiny.** Two quarters of close tracking ≠ a permanent equilibrium. Worth watching.
